## Why Now? (Three Regulatory/Market Tailwinds)

### 1. CMS-HCC Model V28 Transition (Complete in 2026)

**What Changed**:
- 115 HCC categories (up from 86)
- 7,770 valid diagnostic codes (down from 9,797)
- ~3.12% average risk score reduction
- Example: CHF now 5 categories (vs. 1 in V24)

**Market Impact**:
- Health plans re-evaluating vendors
- Existing platforms need re-training, re-validation
- Software that automates V24→V28 migration has strong advantage
- Early adopters get first-mover benefit (less competition in mid-2026)

**Startup Advantage**:
- V28-native platforms can build faster than legacy systems migrating
- No V24 baggage = cleaner code
- Disaffection with incumbent vendors during transition = sales window

---

### 2. RADV Audit Expansion (Aggressive Enforcement 2025-2026)

**The Problem**:
- $19.07B improper payments in MA (2024)
- CMS found 70-90% of certain codes lack supporting documentation
- Prior audits (2011-2013) found 5-8% overpayment rates
- Potential recoupment: $50-$80M+ for mid-sized plan

**CMS Response**:
- Expanding audit team from 40 to 2,000 coders by Sept 2025
- January 2026: All MA plans to be audited (not sample)
- Using AI to identify high-risk codes for manual review
- Court rulings creating uncertainty (Sept 2025 ruled against CMS extrapolation; CMS appealing Nov 2025)

**Market Impact**:
- Health plans desperately need audit-defensible solutions
- "Defensible accuracy" becoming more valuable than "maximum accuracy"
- Evidence chains (sentence-level linking of diagnoses to clinical notes) = new table stake
- Neuro-symbolic AI (transparent, explainable) winning over black-box LLMs

**Startup Advantage**:
- Can build audit-defense-first from day one
- Big Four legacy systems retrofitting audit trails = inefficient
- Early positioning as "RADV-ready" = strong GTM narrative
- This is forced, not discretionary technology upgrade

## How to Compete: Startup Playbook

### Step 1: Choose Your Vertical (Micro-Niche)

**DO NOT**: Build "better Optum"
**DO**: Build "Optum for [specific customer type or use case]"

**Examples**:
- "Optum for ACOs" (like Navina for providers)
- "Optum for smaller health plans" (affordable alternative)
- "Optum for RADV defense" (like RAAPID focused positioning)
- "Optum for prospective capture" (point-of-care focus)

**Selection Criteria**:
- Underserved by Big Four
- Specific problem with known pain point
- Large enough to build $10M+ ARR business
- Regulatory tailwind helping adoption

### Step 2: Build Domain Expertise

**Hire Your First Sales/Product Person**:
- Minimum 10+ years healthcare experience
- Should know "how health plans actually work"
- Preferably former customer or competitor employee
- NOT just AI engineer

**Founding Team Red Flag**:
- All engineers, no healthcare domain expert
- Slows time to market
- Makes sales harder (no customer credibility)

### Step 3: Pick Your AI Approach & Own It

**Don't Be Vague** (e.g., "We use AI/ML")
**Be Specific** (e.g., "We use neuro-symbolic AI with 4M+ clinical entity knowledge graph")

**Options**:
1. **Neuro-Symbolic AI** (RAAPID approach)
   - Requires: Knowledge graph + neural network
   - Positioning: "Defensible accuracy, explainable reasoning"
   - Competitive advantage: RADV audit defense

2. **Hybrid AI** (Reveleer approach)
   - Generative AI + deterministic rules
   - Positioning: "Fewer, more precise recommendations"
   - Competitive advantage: Reduced false positives

3. **Deep Learning** (CodaMetrix approach)
   - Neural networks learning from patterns
   - Positioning: "Automated, continuous improvement"
   - Competitive advantage: Cost reduction

4. **Prospective Clinical AI** (Navina approach)
   - Real-time diagnosis suggestions
   - Positioning: "Discovery at point of care"
   - Competitive advantage: Better outcomes

**Rule**: Pick one approach, be opinionated about it, own the positioning.

### Step 4: Build for Your Customer, Not the Market

**Vatica Example**: Won 3 KLAS Best in Class awards by:
- Making software around how PCPs actually work
- Combining software + services (support required)
- Obsessing over customer success, not feature count
- Building brand through customer advocacy

**Your Focus**:
- Map actual customer workflow (not idealized)
- Build features for that workflow
- Invest heavily in customer success (not sales)
- Let customers become your sales team

### Step 5: Leverage Regulatory Tailwinds

**Use These in GTM**:
1. **V28 Migration** (Ongoing through 2026)
   - "Help us migrate from V24 to V28"
   - "Validate our V28 mapping"
   - Forced change event = sales window

2. **RADV Audit Expansion** (2025-2026, escalating)
   - "Prepare for 100% audit coverage"
   - "Prove every diagnosis with clinical evidence"
   - "RADV-ready platform, not bolted-on"
   - Urgency = shorter sales cycles

3. **DOJ Enforcement** (Ongoing)
   - "Risk adjustment integrity"
   - "Defensible coding practices"
   - Creates compliance pressure

**Don't Wait**: These are forcing functions; timing is now.

- Goal: Prove concept with 1-2 early customers

**Series A**:

## Revenue Model Recommendations

### Pricing Strategy

**Avoid**: Pure per-member-per-month (commoditizes your solution)

**Better Options**:

1. **Flat Annual License + Usage Fee**
   - Base: $100K-$500K/year (depending on plan size)
   - Variable: Per chart reviewed, per diagnosis captured, etc.
   - Pros: Predictable + scales with value
   - Cons: Complex negotiations

2. **Outcome-Based / ROI Sharing**
   - "You get 3:1 ROI, we share in the upside"
   - Pros: Aligns incentives, builds customer confidence
   - Cons: Hard to predict revenue, requires strong unit economics

3. **Per-Member-Per-Month (with floor)**
   - Base: $0.20 PMPM minimum $50K/year
   - Pros: Scales with customer growth
   - Cons: Customers will optimize it down

4. **Specialty Premium Pricing**
   - Base platform: $50K-$200K/year
   - RADV audit module: +$30K
   - Prospective capture: +$50K
   - Pros: Customers pay for specific value
   - Cons: Complex packaging

**Recommendation**: Start with Flat + Usage (option 1) to prove ROI quickly, then move to outcome-based as you scale.

- Trying to sell to top 5 health plans first
- Sales-driven go-to-market (healthcare needs customer success first)

- No healthcare domain expertise on team

- Underestimating RAAPID or Reveleer

- Building something without regulatory tailwind

## Final Thoughts

The RAF/HCC coding market is entering a pivotal moment in 2026. Major regulatory transitions (V28, RADV audits) are creating urgency and forcing re-evaluation cycles. Incumbent platforms have vulnerabilities—they're complex, expensive, and not specialized enough. A well-positioned startup with domain expertise, clear differentiation, and audit-defensible technology can capture meaningful market share in a $6B market.

But this is a specialist's game, not a generalist's. Success requires understanding healthcare deeply, choosing a focused vertical, and obsessing over customer success rather than feature count.

The window to enter this market is now (2026). By 2027-2028, the regulatory tailwinds fade, consolidation continues, and late entrants face steeper challenges.

**The opportunity is real. The market timing is excellent. The competition is formidable. Choose wisely.**
