KEY FINDINGS SUMMARY
================================================================================

SEVEN PRIMARY DATA SOURCES FOR HCC CAPTURE:

1. Laboratory Values (30-40% impact)
   - eGFR for CKD staging (objective validation)
   - HbA1c for diabetes control assessment
   - BNP for heart failure severity
   - AFP for hepatocellular carcinoma
   - Liver function panel for cirrhosis/advanced disease

2. Medication Reconciliation (25-35% impact)
   - Medications reveal likely diagnoses
   - Insulin/Metformin → Diabetes
   - Loop diuretics → Heart failure
   - COPD inhalers → COPD
   - Medications without diagnosis codes = documentation gap

3. Vital Signs & Anthropometrics (15-20% impact)
   - BMI documentation MUST be specific (not calculated)
   - Blood pressure trends validate hypertension severity
   - Weight gain patterns indicate CHF exacerbation
   - Respiratory rate and O2 sat for COPD monitoring

4. Problem Lists (if maintained properly)
   - Foundation for MEAT (Monitor, Evaluate, Assess, Treat) criteria
   - Must reflect CURRENT active conditions only
   - Each problem must have supporting encounter documentation
   - Critical for RADV audit compliance

5. Specialist Referral & Visit Data
   - Cardiology referrals = likely heart disease gaps
   - Nephrology referrals = CKD severity gaps
   - Pulmonology referrals = COPD severity gaps
   - 30-50% reduction in retrospective coding when optimized

6. Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)
   - Currently NOT included in CMS-HCC models
   - Will likely be incorporated in future models
   - Early adopters gain advantage when changes occur
   - Explains medication/treatment compliance challenges

7. Immunization Records & Quality Data
   - HEDIS alignment connects quality to risk adjustment
   - Preventive care gaps may indicate disease complexity
   - Supporting documentation for condition severity

REGULATORY LANDSCAPE (2025-2026):

RAPS (Legacy) → EDPS (Current) Transition:
- 100% of 2025+ DOS now use EDPS
- EDPS has 7-layer validation vs. RAPS 2-layer
- Average revenue impact: -11.9% if not prepared

CMS V28 Implementation (100% in 2025):
- 115 HCCs (vs. 86 in V24)
- 29 NEW HCC categories

- Example: "Diabetes" must be Type 1 vs. Type 2

RADV Audit Expansion:

- Aetna/CVS settlement: $117.7M (March 2026) for non-compliance
- Active CMS enforcement ongoing

- After Phase 4: 30-40% improvement (sustained)

MEAT Criteria Implementation:

MEAT = Monitor, Evaluate, Assess/Address, Treat
(Only ONE element needed; more = stronger documentation)

M = Objective monitoring: "Lab reviewed, eGFR 32"
E = Assessment of findings: "eGFR declined from 45 to 32—Stage 4"
A = Clinical decision: "Discussed with patient, referred to nephrology"
T = Active management: "Increased lisinopril from 10 to 20 mg"

Documentation Quality Scoring:
- Level 4 (3+ MEAT elements): AUDIT-SAFE ✓
- Level 3 (2 MEAT elements): ACCEPTABLE
- Level 2 (1 MEAT element): REQUIRES IMPROVEMENT
- Level 1 (No MEAT): VERY HIGH AUDIT RISK ✗

Target: 60%+ of HCC coding at Level 4

CRITICAL SUCCESS FACTORS
================================================================================

1. TECHNOLOGY + PEOPLE ALIGNMENT
   - Tech alone = 30% impact
   - Tech + change management = 80% impact
   - Need clinical champion + coder buy-in + provider engagement

2. DATA QUALITY FOUNDATION
   - Must clean problem lists BEFORE implementing coding system
   - Must validate medication/lab data quality
   - Must establish clear documentation standards (MEAT)

3. GOVERNANCE & COMPLIANCE
   - Establish audit trails from day one
   - Implement bidirectional chart review (add AND remove)
   - Create escalation paths for unsupported diagnoses

4. MEASUREMENT & ACCOUNTABILITY
   - Track HCC capture rates quarterly
   - Benchmark against peers
   - Tie provider incentives to BOTH quality AND risk accuracy
   - Communicate wins to maintain momentum

5. PROVIDER EDUCATION
   - Training on MEAT and V28 requirements
   - Regular feedback on individual/group performance
   - Make it about BETTER PATIENT CARE (not just money)
   - Provide tools and templates to make documentation easier
