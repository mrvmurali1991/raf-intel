## CROSS-VERIFICATION METHODOLOGY

### Fact Verification Process
1. **Primary Source Confirmation:** Whenever possible, verified facts against official CMS/HHS/DOJ sources
2. **Multiple Source Triangulation:** Key claims verified across 2-3 independent sources
3. **Date Confirmation:** All dates cross-checked against official announcements and court documents
4. **Penalty Amount Verification:** Settlement amounts verified against DOJ press releases
5. **Regulatory Citation Validation:** CFR citations confirmed against eCFR and Cornell Law

### Conflict Resolution
When sources conflicted:
- Prioritized official government sources over secondary analysis
- Used most recent sources for evolving legal standards
- Documented divergent interpretations (e.g., FFS adjuster debate between CMS and courts)
- Noted procedural status of vacated/appealed rules

## RESEARCH GAPS AND LIMITATIONS

### Acknowledged Gaps
1. **UnitedHealth Settlement Terms:** Investigation ongoing as of April 2026; final settlement amount and terms not yet public
2. **Humana v. Becerra Appeal Outcome:** Appeal filed November 2025; final ruling expected 6-12 months out
3. **2026 RADV Audit Results:** Current year audits ongoing; final extrapolation numbers not yet available
4. **Individual OIG CIA Terms:** Some Corporate Integrity Agreements (Cigna, Aetna) have not fully detailed provisions published
5. **Specific CMS-HCC V28 Error Rates:** Historical error rate data (20-40% invalidation) from V24; V28 specific rates still emerging

### Limitations of Available Data
- Enforcement data lags actual violations by 12-24+ months
- Published settlements represent known cases; unknown/under-investigation cases not documented
- Regulatory guidance sometimes issued in piecemeal fashion (HPMS memos, FAQs) rather than consolidated documents
- Court decisions ongoing; procedural uncertainty creates interpretation gaps

### Information Not Available
- Confidential enforcement investigation details (UnitedHealth, others)
- Non-public settlement negotiations and rejected settlement proposals
- Proprietary vendor methodologies for diagnosis capture
- Internal MA organization compliance audit results (proprietary)

## RESEARCH QUALITY ASSURANCE

### Methodology Review
- All searches conducted using domain-specific terminology
- Boolean operators and phrase searches used for precision
- Multiple search variations tested to ensure comprehensive coverage
- Redundant searches performed to identify overlooked sources

### Citation Accuracy
- All regulatory citations verified against official eCFR
- Court decision URLs linked to Georgetown Litigation Tracker and official court sources
- Settlement amounts cross-checked against DOJ official press releases
- Dates verified against multiple authoritative sources

### Currency Assessment
- Research reflects information available as of April 1, 2026
- Future-dated guidance noted appropriately (e.g., "proposed rule" vs. "final rule")
- Appeal statuses marked as current as of April 2026
- Ongoing investigations noted as status as of last public announcement

### Bias Mitigation
- Balanced representation of multiple perspectives (e.g., CMS extrapolation authority vs. Humana challenge)
- Vendor marketing materials excluded from analysis
- Conflicting estimates (CMS $17B vs. MedPAC $43B overpayment) both presented with source attribution
- Enforcement actions presented factually without advocacy

## CONCLUSION

This research provides a comprehensive, current assessment of CMS Risk Adjustment regulatory requirements and compliance landscape as of April 2026. The findings are based on 80+ authoritative sources including official government documents, court decisions, and expert legal analysis. The research identifies a significantly intensified enforcement environment with $556M+ settlements, dramatic audit expansion (60 to 550+ contracts), new OIG guidance baseline, and ongoing judicial uncertainty regarding extrapolation methodology.

The delivered documents provide executives, compliance officers, legal counsel, and operational teams with the specific regulatory citations, compliance frameworks, enforcement patterns, and strategic priorities necessary to design and implement effective risk adjustment compliance programs.

Key takeaway: Risk adjustment is no longer a coding back-office function—it is an enterprise-level compliance imperative requiring C-suite engagement, comprehensive internal controls, third-party oversight, and continuous monitoring of evolving regulatory standards.
