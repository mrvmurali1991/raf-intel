## CRITICAL REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS

### Statutory Framework
- **42 CFR § 422.310:** MA organizations must submit complete, accurate risk adjustment data characterizing context and purpose of items/services
- **31 U.S.C. § 3729 (False Claims Act):** Every unsupported diagnosis code = false claim; treble damages + $11,000-$15,000 per violation
- **42 CFR § 422.501:** MA organizations required to maintain compliance programs with seven core elements
- **Social Security Act § 1128(b):** OIG authority to exclude individuals/entities; monthly LEIE screening required

### Data Submission Deadlines
- **First Friday in September:** Services from 12-month period ending June 30
- **First Friday in March:** Services from 12-month period ending December 31
- **Enforcement Note:** Data submitted after deadline not used in payment calculations; false data triggers penalties

### Documentation Standard: MEAT Framework
**Every HCC code must be supported by documentation showing:**
- **Monitoring:** Active ongoing monitoring of the condition
- **Evaluation:** Clinical evaluation and assessment
- **Assessment:** Documented clinical assessment that condition is present
- **Treatment:** Treatment plan linked to the disease process

Historical audit data: 20-40% of unsupported HCCs invalidated during RADV audits

## FALSE CLAIMS ACT EXPOSURE

### Prohibited Practices (DOJ Enforcement Priorities)
1. **One-Way Audits:** Adding diagnosis codes but never removing invalid ones (Kaiser penalty: $556M)
2. **Post-Encounter Addenda:** Diagnoses added months/years after encounter (Kaiser had 500,000 such cases)
3. **Unsupported High-Dollar HCCs:** Codes lacking MEAT documentation
4. **Morbid Obesity Without BMI:** Obesity diagnoses without documented body mass index support (Aetna: $117.7M)
5. **Aggressive Chart Review:** Vendor-driven diagnosis capture without clinical validation (Cigna: $172M)

### FCA Liability Calculation Example
- 500,000 unsupported diagnosis codes = 500,000 false claims
- At $11,000-$15,000 per claim = $5.5-7.5 billion in penalties alone
- PLUS treble damages on actual overpayments (3x actual damages)
- **Result:** $1 billion overpayment could generate $3-9 billion in total liability

## DOCUMENTATION STANDARDS BY HCC CATEGORY

### High-Risk HCCs (OIG December 2023 Toolkit Focus)
1. Diabetes complications (complex interactions with other conditions)
2. Heart failure and cardiovascular disease
3. Chronic kidney disease stages
4. COPD/respiratory conditions
5. Neurological conditions
6. Morbid obesity (requires BMI documentation)
7. Mental health/behavioral diagnoses

**Compliance Standard:** These codes receive heightened audit scrutiny; require robust, specific documentation support

### Chronic Condition Recertification
- Must be evaluated and documented annually
- Cannot rely on historical documentation from prior years
- Treatment must be actively linked to disease management
- Problem lists must reflect current status

## COMPLIANCE PROGRAM REQUIREMENTS (OIG 2026 Guidance)

### Seven Core Elements
1. **Written Policies & Procedures** - Updated annually, distributed to all staff
2. **Designated Compliance Officer** - Reports to senior management; oversees program
3. **Training & Education** - Annual minimum; risk adjustment-specific content
4. **Internal Monitoring & Auditing** - Pre- and post-submission data audits
5. **Reporting & Investigation** - Hotline/reporting mechanisms; prompt investigation
6. **Corrective Action** - Timely remediation; overpayment reporting to CMS
7. **Disciplinary Enforcement** - Clear guidelines; consistent application

### Risk Adjustment-Specific Controls (Per OIG Guidance)
- Special scrutiny for high-risk HCC codes
- Pair diagnosis capture with clinical quality controls
- Vendor oversight and auditing
- Coder credential verification (CRC recommended)
- Benchmarking against national HCC prevalence
- Monitoring incentive structures (ensure not rewarding unsupported codes)
- Encounter data quality validation

## ENCOUNTER DATA COMPLETENESS STANDARDS

### Submission Requirements (42 CFR § 422.310)
- Data must characterize context and purpose of items/services
- Must conform to CMS standards equivalent to Medicare FFS data
- Must follow ANSI 837 5010 format
- Must meet CMS' data quality specifications

### Quality Benchmarks
**High Completeness Standards:**
- Less than 10% missing hospital stays
- Less than ±10% difference in ambulatory/ED visits (vs. HEDIS data)
- Complete supporting documentation for all diagnosis codes

**Current Industry Status:**
- Only 83 of 380 MA contracts (21.8%) with 2,500+ enrollees meet high completeness standards
- Home health and nursing home encounter files noted as particularly inaccurate
- Significant plan-level variation across industry

## PENALTY FRAMEWORK

### Civil Monetary Penalties (CMP)
**Per Affected Beneficiary:** Up to $25,000 (as adjusted annually under 45 CFR Part 102)

**Ongoing Non-Compliance:** Additional $10,000 per week after CMS notice (as adjusted annually)

**2025 Penalty Range:** $5,800 to $2,000,000 depending on violation severity and beneficiary count

### False Claims Act Penalties
- **Treble Damages:** 3x government's actual damages
- **Per-Claim Penalty:** $11,000-$15,000 per violation (escalates with inflation)
- **Exclusion:** Authority to exclude from Federal healthcare programs (OIG List of Excluded Individuals/Entities - LEIE)

## CMS-HCC MODEL TRANSITION (2024-2026)

### V28 Model Implementation
- **2024:** 67% current (V24) + 33% V28
- **2025:** 33% current + 67% V28
- **2026:** 100% V28 (current status)

### V28 Changes Affecting Compliance
- Increased HCCs: 86 (V24) → 115 (V28)
- Removed codes: 2,294 ICD-10-CM codes no longer map to payment HCCs
- Constrained coefficients: HCCs with discretionary coding variation limited
- Purpose: Reduce MA coding intensity variation relative to FFS

## SEPTEMBER 2025 COURT RULING: HUMANA V. BECERRA

### Decision Summary
**September 25, 2025:** Federal court (N.D. Texas) vacated CMS's 2023 RADV Final Rule (CMS-1733-F) on Administrative Procedure Act (APA) procedural grounds.

### Court's Reasoning
- CMS violated APA by failing to adequately explain departure from FFS adjuster reasoning presented in 2018 Proposed Rule
- 2023 Final Rule adopted new justifications not logically outgrown from proposed rule comments
- Decision did NOT address substantive legality of extrapolation or FFS adjuster elimination

### Current Status
- CMS appealed November 21, 2025
- Extrapolation methodology in procedural limbo pending appeal
- Uncertainty likely 6-12 months from appeal filing
- Organizations must prepare for both scenarios

### Implications
- **If Appeal Succeeds:** Extrapolation authority reinstated; RADV exposure multiplies
- **If Appeal Fails:** CMS must reissue rule with stronger procedural foundation; timeline extends
- **Either Way:** RADV audits continue under pre-2023 methodology while litigation pending

## BOTTOM LINE FOR EXECUTIVES

**Risk adjustment compliance is no longer a back-office coding issue—it is now a top-tier enterprise risk.**

1. **Enforcement is intensifying:** CMS expanded from 60 to 550+ audits with 2,000+ reviewers; FY 2025 healthcare fraud recovery exceeded $5.7 billion with MA risk adjustment as top DOJ priority.

2. **Settlements are massive:** Kaiser ($556M), Cigna ($172M), Aetna ($117.7M) demonstrate that DOJ will pursue aggressive FCA theories with billion-dollar-scale penalties. One unsupported diagnosis code = one false claim.

3. **Documentation failures are catastrophic:** 20-40% of unsupported codes fail audit; historical patterns show one-way audits, post-encounter addenda, and absent MEAT documentation trigger enforcement.

4. **Procedural uncertainty exists but won't delay enforcement:** Humana v. Becerra vacated the extrapolation rule on procedural grounds, but CMS appealed and audits continue. Expect extrapolation to ultimately be allowed with procedural refinements.

5. **Compliance guidance is now definitive:** OIG's February 2026 ICPG—first update in 25 years—sets new expectations including vendor oversight, coder credentials, pre/post-submission audits, and benchmarking. Non-compliance is indefensible.

**Strategic Imperative:** Immediately implement comprehensive compliance program meeting OIG 2026 standards, eliminate all one-way audit practices, establish rigorous MEAT documentation validation, and prepare for imminent RADV audits under assumption that extrapolation will be allowed.
